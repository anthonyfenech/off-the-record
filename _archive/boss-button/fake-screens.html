Off the Record (book)

Off the Record is a memoir by American sports journalist Anthony Fenech, chronicling his fifteen years covering the Detroit Tigers for the Detroit Free Press. The book offers an insider's perspective on the inner workings of a Major League Baseball beat, the relationships between journalists and players, and the dramatic moments that defined an era of Tigers baseball.

Contents
  1. Background
  2. Content
  3. Reception
  4. See also
  5. References

Background

Fenech began covering the Detroit Tigers in 2015, joining the Detroit Free Press as a beat reporter. Over the following years, he covered the team through multiple seasons, playoff runs, rebuild efforts, and various controversies within the organization.[1]

The title refers to the journalism practice of receiving information "off the record"—meaning the source does not want to be identified or quoted directly. The book explores how these confidential conversations shape sports coverage.

Content

The memoir covers major events in Tigers history during Fenech's tenure, including the team's transition from a competitive roster featuring Miguel Cabrera to a rebuilding phase. The book discusses the trade deadline decisions, clubhouse dynamics, and the relationships between beat reporters and team personnel.

Notable chapters cover the author's experiences at Comerica Park, spring training in Lakeland, Florida, and road trips throughout Major League Baseball.

Reception

The book has been noted for its candid portrayal of sports journalism and the challenges of maintaining objectivity while building sources.[2] [citation needed]
