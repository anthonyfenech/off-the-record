Test 1: Verlander Camera Confrontation

CONTEXT: Spring training 2017. Verlander just blew you off after sitting in the heat for an hour. Now you're in a press scrum with TV cameras rolling.

You spent two years building this relationship. He knows he's blowing you off - keeping you in your place. The beat writers are crowded around. Channel 2 and Channel 4 have their cameras zooming in.

You know Verlander cares deeply about his on-camera image more than anything...

[This is where you would call him out on camera in the actual book]

"I asked it too early and immediately looked at the camera guys, spread my hands and said, 'Don't use that, guys.'"

Test 2: Verlander Trade Tweet

CONTEXT: August 31, 2017, 12:16 AM. Your phone just rang. The source confirmed it.

This is the BIGGEST scoop of your career. Justin Verlander traded to Houston. You asked instantly, hurriedly: "Is it done?" They said yes, the deal is done.

You have 11 seconds before other reporters get it. Twitter is open. Your fingers are on the keyboard.

Other reporters are circling. This could make or break your reputation...

[This is where you would tweet the scoop in the actual book]

"I hit Send and I tweeted, 'The Tigers have traded Justin Verlander to the Astros,' told it before anyone else on the planet."

Mother-fucking WOOOOO!!!!!

Test 3: Astros Clubhouse Decision

CONTEXT: August 24, 2019. Day after the Verlander Incident.

Verlander called you unethical on national television. ESPN ran the story. The Astros might bar you from the clubhouse.

But MLB rules guarantee reporters equal access. You're the Bad Boy of Ball Writing now. Do you walk into that clubhouse?

Your editor is watching. Your peers are watching. Verlander is watching...

[This is where you would walk into the clubhouse in the actual book]

"I walked right the fuck in. History will be on your side."

Test 4: How Should I Quit?

CONTEXT: July 20, 2020. Morning.

You woke up feeling dangerous today. You've been frozen out, humiliated, pushed to the breaking point by Tigers PR and your own editor.

On your notepad: Jet's Pizza phone number. Comerica Park address. Ron Colangelo's direct line.

You texted Deep Throat at 10:49 AM: "If you don't think I'll order Jet's Pizza to deliver to Tigers PR as a thank you for their help this summer camp..."

How do you actually quit this job?

[This is where you would order the pizza in the actual book]

"I ordered two large pizzas, cheese bread, and a two-liter of Coke."

"I sent them *pizza*."

Test 5: V-Mart Hospital Text

CONTEXT: August 27, 2017, 11:30 PM.

You've spent days reporting an explosive story: Victor Martinez is causing clubhouse dysfunction. The story is edited, fact-checked, ready to publish at 2 AM.

You're at the Irish bar, brought up two shots of whiskey. It's nearing 11:30 PM when you receive a most unnerving text.

It's from Brad Ausmus, the manager of the team: "U might want to hold off on ur story."

You have 2.5 hours until publication. What do you text back?

[This is where you would text Ausmus in the actual book]

"What the fuck, man?"

Ausmus revealed V-Mart went to the hospital with heart issues. The story was killed. Disaster averted.

(REPORTER'S NOTE: I have never independently confirmed that Martinez actually went to the hospital that night. The timing could seem "exceptionally coincidental.")
