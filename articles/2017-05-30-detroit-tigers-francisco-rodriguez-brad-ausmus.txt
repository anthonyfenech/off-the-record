DATE: 2017-05-30

KANSAS CITY, Mo. &ndash; The Francisco Rodriguez conundrum continued Monday night.

With the Tigers holding the slimmest of leads in the bottom of the seventh inning against the Royals, manager Brad Ausmus called on the veteran right-hander to protect a one-run lead for the first time since

It did not go well. Rodriguez coughed up the lead by allowing a two-run home run to Eric Hosmer. It was his sixth home run allowed this season and fifth against a left-handed hitter.

But Ausmus was not forced to use Rodriguez: It was his choice.

&ldquo;To be honest with you, I was probably going to use (Rodriguez) in that situation anyways. I wasn&rsquo;t forced by that,&rdquo; Ausmus said. &ldquo;How he&rsquo;s pitched, how he&rsquo;s been throwing, normally guys like him are more effective when the game is on the line. He&rsquo;s always pitched like that."

And then, Ausmus detailed the biggest reason: &ldquo;I think we have to find out if he can pitch with the game on the line, quite frankly.&rdquo;

Because if Rodriguez cannot, the Tigers likely don&rsquo;t have a spot for him on the team.

Rodriguez, 35, is in the midst of his worst professional season. He is not fooling hitters and has among the worst numbers in the league. But his impact on the bullpen is so big off the field, and his track record so lengthy on it, that the Tigers will make absolutely sure he cannot be counted on in any situation before cutting him loose.

&ldquo;Even after tonight, I still think we&rsquo;re going to have to test him to make sure,&rdquo; Ausmus said Monday. &ldquo;Maybe it&rsquo;s a tie game or maybe it&rsquo;s a one-run deficit because we need to. Just a close game, I would say.&rdquo;

Ausmus&rsquo; words are likely baffling to a fan base which has not seen any noticeable improvement in Rodriguez throughout the season. In 19 games this season, Rodriguez has posted a 7.79 ERA and 1.78 WHIP. He is 2-5 with five blown saves.

It&rsquo;s almost as simple as this: If Rodriguez, who has pitched in high-leverage situations throughout his career, can&rsquo;t anymore, then the Tigers cannot play short-handed in their bullpen. For a team hoping to contend in the American League postseason picture, playing a man short in the bullpen &ndash; which they would be if Rodriguez can only pitch in mop-up situations &ndash; is too much.

&ldquo;We said it when we took him out of the closer&rsquo;s role,&rdquo; Ausmus said. &ldquo;We&rsquo;re better if he&rsquo;s on the team and pitching well, we&rsquo;re a better bullpen. We&rsquo;re trying to do what we can to get him back.&rdquo;

Before Monday night&rsquo;s game, Rodriguez did not pitch since May 21 against the Rangers, a span of eight days.

&ldquo;He wouldn&rsquo;t make excuses and I&rsquo;m not going to make excuses,&rdquo; Ausmus said. &ldquo;The layoff&rsquo;s not ideal, but we&rsquo;re going to stay the course.&rdquo;

Looming large in a potential decision this season in Rodriguez&rsquo;s standing is his impact on the team&rsquo;s young pitchers.

Shane Greene, who has been taken under his wing, said recently: &ldquo;(Rodriguez) is our most important pitcher in the bullpen, whether he pitches or not.&rdquo;

Ausmus wouldn&rsquo;t term that side of it complicated but did say, &ldquo;You&rsquo;ll get a good understanding of why we want him part of it. He is huge down there. He is the leader of the bullpen and has been since he stepped foot into the Tigers clubhouse.

&ldquo;The guy&rsquo;s an unbelievable professional, does things the right way, very unselfish. He&rsquo;s without question the guy that everyone looks to, to be a steadying and influential mentor. He just is.&rdquo;

But, as Ausmus has said ad nauseam throughout his four years as manager, baseball is a performance-driven game. Right now, Rodriguez&rsquo;s performance is sorely lacking. Still, the team is trying to get him back aboard by seeing if he can pass the late-inning tests that will follow.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;ll figure that out if he can&rsquo;t,&rdquo; Ausmus said. &ldquo;There&rsquo;s no decision being made on that.&rdquo;
