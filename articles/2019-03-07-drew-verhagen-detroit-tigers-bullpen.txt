Asked if the team could specify those general tests, a media relations official said the tests were arm-related.

VerHagen, who was very effective in his first three Grapefruit League appearances, was not able to escape the fifth inning on Wednesday, allowing four runs on three walks and two hits.

Afterwards, pitching coach Rick Anderson said he was dealing with &ldquo;dead arm&rdquo; symptoms.

Upon VerHagen&rsquo;s exit, he was joined by general manager Al Avila and head athletic trainer Doug Teter for a conversation inside the training room at Champion Field.

The scene initiated an almost-immediate policy change for the Tigers, whose media relations department sent out a midday Thursday e-mail to reporters, saying they were no longer permitted in the clubhouse during games.

In years past &ndash; dating back well before the Joker Marchant Stadium renovations in 2017 &ndash; the clubhouse opened to reporters when the game&rsquo;s starting pitcher was ready to speak to the media, affording access to other starting players when leaving the game.

Questions about the policy change were directed to media relations director Chad Crunk, who said that &ldquo;many teams&rdquo; across Major League Baseball enact the same policy.

MLB's media policy does not require clubhouses to open during spring training games.

However, two dozen media relations officials and reporters across the league polled by the Free Press coincide with one national baseball reporter&rsquo;s view that limiting clubhouse access in such a fashion is &ldquo;extremely rare.&rdquo;

Of the 20 teams polled, only four operate in such a fashion, with two of those teams saying they simply don&rsquo;t have the man power. Consistently, the Tigers have had three media relations officials on-hand throughout spring training.

Nevertheless, that fit of paranoia did not permeate Gardenhire&rsquo;s office, though he also did not have many answers for VerHagen&rsquo;s situation.

&ldquo;I hate that word. &lsquo;Dead arm&rsquo; means you&rsquo;re not throwing the ball good. He just didn&rsquo;t have it the other day.&rdquo;

VerHagen, 28, is coming off a strong second half in 2018, in which he posted a 2.61 ERA and 1.032 WHIP in 24 appearances, with 29 strikeouts in 31 innings. If he is forced to miss significant time, putting his status for Opening Day in jeopardy, it would be a sizeable blow to the Tigers&rsquo; bullpen.

Still, given the information &ndash; or lack thereof &ndash; on hand, it is too early to pass judgement on the severity.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;re just making sure everything&rsquo;s going good, everything&rsquo;s right,&rdquo; Gardenhire said. &ldquo;They run some tests, check him, just like they do everybody. Just general.&rdquo;
