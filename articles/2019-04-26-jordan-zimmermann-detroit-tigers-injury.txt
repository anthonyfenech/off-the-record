The diagnosis, Zimmermann said, was probably a best-case scenario. Compared to the worst-case scenario, head athletic trainer Doug Teter said, it was good news.

Zimmermann will be shut down from throwing for up to a week, Teter said, and the timeline for his return &mdash; with many variables attached to it &mdash; is 3-4 weeks.

&ldquo;This is going to be a &lsquo;take it as it comes,&rsquo;&rdquo; Teter said. &ldquo;It&rsquo;s not cut and dry. Zimmermann left Thursday night&rsquo;s start after three innings. He said he first experienced the discomfort while warming up and thought it was something he could pitch through.

&ldquo;And I was out there throwing, pitch-by-pitch, it just kept gradually getting worse and worse,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;And I knew something wasn&rsquo;t right.&rdquo;

Zimmermann had felt a similar pain nearly 10 years before, which led to elbow ligament reconstruction surgery. The prospect of a second such surgery made the hours leading up to the MRI stressful, with perhaps &mdash; in the most worst-case of scenarios &mdash; his career in the balance.

Elbow ligament reconstruction surgery generally requires 12-to-14 months of recovery time. The process is long and arduous, and Zimmermann, now 31 years old, is much older than he was when first undergoing the operation.

About the possibility of surgery looming, Zimmermann said, &ldquo;That&rsquo;s why I was kind of on the fence for what the MRI was going to say today. It was, like I said, the best-case scenario.&rdquo;

The &ldquo;facts of the case,&rdquo; as presented by Teter, are there is a sprain to the UCL ligament and damage to the ligament itself. In team doctor Stephen Lemos&rsquo; opinion, there was not enough to consider surgery for the time being. Zimmermann does not plan to seek a second opinion.

&ldquo;Right now, it&rsquo;s a conservative rehab program,&rdquo; Teter said.

Though the diagnosis was deemed good news, there are still many hurdles to clear. Zimmermann will progress in a throwing program, from 60 feet to 120 feet to a bullpen session. It is unknown how his arm will respond to rehabilitation, meaning that surgery &mdash; although not at the moment &mdash; could return to the table.

&ldquo;With any rehab, you&rsquo;re going forward throwing and you&rsquo;re having setbacks, keep having setbacks, can&rsquo;t get over the hump, then obviously you have to re-adjust and look where you&rsquo;re at, at that time,&rdquo; Teter said. &ldquo;So, sure.
