OMAHA, Neb. &mdash; A TrackMan device was sitting in an office in the Michigan baseball department when Chris Fetter arrived, armed with information from one of the major leagues&rsquo; most advanced teams, two years before he would finally make it to Omaha.

Fetter was close before, part of U-M&rsquo;s last closest team, in 2007, when they advanced to the Super Regionals in dramatic fashion. That year, Alan Oaks blasted the most forgetful pitch of David Price&rsquo;s college career to upend No. 1 Vanderbilt in the regional final in Nashville.

After a diverse decade in baseball after that, Fetter is back in Ann Arbor, now in his second season as pitching coach, and credited with guiding the Wolverines&rsquo; pitchers to the College World Series,

&ldquo;He&rsquo;s one of the greatest minds in college baseball right now, especially when it comes to pitching,&rdquo; sophomore right-hander Jeff Criswell said. &ldquo;I think his background with the Dodgers really took him to the next level.&rdquo;

Fetter, 33, returned to his alma mater two summers ago, after spending six months with the Dodgers as their minor-league pitching coordinator. Before that, he was the Ball State pitching coach for one season, and a professional scout with the Angels for three seasons.

Fetter is U-M's all-time innings pitched leader (332⅓). He was drafted in the ninth round by the Padres and after four unfruitful years in the minor leagues, stayed on board with the organization as a player-coach with the Double-A San Antonio, which was his unofficial beginning to coaching.

&ldquo;To get a chance to come back home, it&rsquo;s my alma mater, they trust me to come in with their pitchers,  I felt like this was a no-brainer,&rdquo; Fetter said. &ldquo;If it wasn&rsquo;t Michigan, it wasn&rsquo;t going to be any other program but Michigan. It was the only place I would have left the Dodgers for.&rdquo;

Fetter thought he was well-versed on the analytical side of the game &ndash; until he joined the Dodgers. With L.A., he learned how to evaluate numbers with the most advanced technology, and has made that a focal point of the Wolverines&rsquo; pitching development.

At the time Fetter returned, U-M was in the final stages of readying the TrackMan, which measures things like spin rate and launch angle, after the legal red tape on how the information would be disseminated was unwrapped.

&ldquo;With their arsenals, we just tried to build each individual player and just kind of iron those things out,&rdquo; Fetter said. &ldquo;Just help them understand who they are to a greater level.

&ldquo;If I know what pitches I have that separate me from the average, then I should go out to the mound and be the most confident version of myself.&rdquo;
