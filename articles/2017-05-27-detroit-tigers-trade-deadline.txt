CHICAGO &ndash; The urgency is evident.

It&rsquo;s a feeling in the air, a little bit later this May than last. This time, the players are the subject of pressure and not the manager. And that pressure has officially been turned up on these Detroit Tigers.

It was turned up on Friday morning, when

Within that message &ndash; a wholly unsurprising one, given the Tigers&rsquo; current play and future state &ndash; was a message from the front office to the team. That message was clear: You guys need to start playing better, and soon.

It&rsquo;s fair to question whether it needed to be delivered at this point of the season, as they slog through the toughest road trip of the season at a time in which the team looks to be pressing to get even the smallest portion of offense on the board.

But it was a message nonetheless, a message that the Tigers players &ndash; especially the ones whose immediate futures lay in the balance of the success of this team &ndash; know all too well.

There is a big bunch of blame to be partitioned as of late: The offense deserves the brunt of it. The starting pitching has been worse than the bullpen. The schedule they&rsquo;ve had to play certainly hasn&rsquo;t helped. And it&rsquo;s hard to imagine this crop of past their prime players ever staying as healthy as the Tigers need them to be.

The sentiment heading into this 10-day, 11-game road trip was that the Tigers just needed to survive it. They knew what was in store.

&ldquo;We knew May was going to be tough for us,&rdquo; Miguel Cabrera said earlier in the week. &ldquo;If we can handle May, I think we&rsquo;re going to be OK the rest of the season.&rdquo;

Thus far, they haven&rsquo;t. They are sinking at a time they need to prove to the front office that they can swim. And now they must try to right the ship of this road trip without Kinsler, who counts as perhaps their most important player.

Kinsler&rsquo;s impact is simple: With him, the Tigers might be a winning team. Without him, they absolutely are not.

&ldquo;I think it&rsquo;s gotta be done by June or July, really,&rdquo; J.D. Martinez said during the last home stand. &ldquo;Probably the beginning of July. Because if not, there&rsquo;s a good chance that there&rsquo;s a couple of us that might not be here.&rdquo;

And there&rsquo;s an equally as good chance that if that time comes, the returns for those couple players will be more underwhelming than many believe. General manager Al Avila didn&rsquo;t want to pull the plug last winter. If they lurk in the postseason race, he might find the package to pull the plug this summer.

But nearly 50 games into the season, the Tigers don&rsquo;t resemble anything close to a postseason team and chances are good that the front office agrees.

They&rsquo;re over-reliant on young pitchers. Their bullpen could use two more reliable arms. They have a bunch of big names but a number of them have faded and while they have the same kinds of warts many teams do, theirs seem to show up on an almost nightly basis. They don&rsquo;t play good defense and can&rsquo;t run the bases. There&rsquo;s just something off about this team.

Could it change? Certainly. They have the talent to turn the tide. I just wouldn&rsquo;t hold my breath.

The past couple days have carried the same air as the middle of last May, when the Tigers were probably a loss or two away from costing manager Brad Ausmus his job. There was urgency, a tension, a pressing on a daily basis to get things turned around.

This year, the manager isn&rsquo;t on the hot seat. Some of the players are. And the front office is beginning to send a message of urgency to the team.
