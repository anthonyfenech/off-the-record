&ldquo;I was talking with the trainers in there all this week and saying, &lsquo;The strength&rsquo;s there, the pain&rsquo;s gone, all we need to work on is explosiveness,&rsquo;&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;That&rsquo;s something we haven&rsquo;t really been focused on because when we&rsquo;re coming back from surgery, you&rsquo;re working to strengthen all of those muscles.

But what if, after three surgeries, that explosiveness is no longer there? And if Fulmer cannot reclaim that powerful fastball &mdash; he recorded a career-high in fastball velocity last season, at an average of 96.5 mph, according to FanGraphs &mdash; how closely can he resemble the front-of-the-rotation pitcher of the past?

Velocity is not everything, obviously, but there is a significant difference between 96 mph and 92 mph. Fulmer&rsquo;s calling card has been that fastball. And, while early, it has been nowhere to be seen thus far.

&ldquo;Trust me, I&rsquo;d rather my velocity be where it&rsquo;s at today and get outs than my velocity be normal and not get outs,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;I&rsquo;m all about trying to command the ball and the movement on my pitches more than the velocity. He faced five Braves starters twice.
