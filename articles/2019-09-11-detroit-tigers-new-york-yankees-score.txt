BYLINE: Anthony Fenech
DATE: 2019-09-11
URL: https://www.freep.com/story/sports/mlb/tigers/2019/09/11/detroit-tigers-new-york-yankees-score/2191755001/

It could have been the Staten Island Yankees, down in Class A, for all Ron Gardenhire cared.

No, after the Tigers beat the real Yankees &mdash; the MLB ones, located in the Bronx and pretty close to locking up the AL East title &mdash; in a back-and-forth slugfest,

&ldquo;I think we&rsquo;ve been saying it over and over again, about how we&rsquo;ve been playing good baseball, but only on the road,&rdquo; Gardenhire said.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;ve been doing some of those things, just not at home, so it&rsquo;s really nice to do it at home and see our fans cheer. It really is. Probably the biggest take out of the whole game.&rdquo;

Down 6-0 after the second inning, the Tigers battled their way back into the game. They hit singles &mdash; 14 of their 16 hits were singles &mdash; in response to the Yankees' six home runs (amid 11 hits). They came back once, twice, three times before veteran Jordy Mercer hit the last of those singles into shallow right-center field for a 12-11 walk-off win.

The Tigers beat the Yankees for the third time in five tries this year.

&ldquo;That was interesting, that&rsquo;s for sure,&rdquo; Mercer said.

The walk-off was his second of the season. And though it hasn&rsquo;t been the best year for Mercer &mdash;

&ldquo;Huge for us,&rdquo; Gardenhire said.

The manager has had to rely on so many young players this season. The moment could have fallen to a youngster, overwhelmed by the moment.

Not Mercer, who fell behind in the count against righty Chance Adams and then worked it to 2-2 before flipping a pitch into the outfield for the win.

&ldquo;I was just trying to slow down, not get too excited and try to get something to hit,&rdquo; Mercer said.

When he did, Willi Castro &mdash; who pinch-ran for catcher Grayson Greiner after he doubled into the left-field corner with one out &mdash; scored for the win.

&ldquo;I think (Greiner) deserves more credit than anybody,&rdquo; Mercer said. &ldquo;If it wasn&rsquo;t for him starting it, I don&rsquo;t think we&rsquo;d be talking about it, really.&rdquo;

To be fair, especially after trailing 6-0 after two innings, nobody thought they would be talking about the Tigers beating the Yankees.

&ldquo;A lot of people were counting us out this series,&rdquo; Mercer said. &ldquo;With a group of young kids like that, you just have to keep battling, clawing, and knowing that good things can happen if you keep playing.&rdquo;

&bull; Harold Castro, who keeps hitting, with a 3-for-5 night.

&bull; Victor Reyes, who extended a career-long hitting streak to 12 games.

&bull; Greiner and third baseman Jeimer Candelario, who each had three hits.

&bull; Christin Stewart, who hit a solo home run in the bottom of the fifth inning.

"We&rsquo;ve been doing this,&rdquo; Gardenhire said. &ldquo;We&rsquo;ve been having these days. We&rsquo;ve had really good days at the plate and we put together good at-bats and we battled.&rdquo;

Luckily, the Tigers had one of their few remaining veterans in the batter&rsquo;s box with a chance to win the game in the ninth.

&ldquo;A lot of guys would foul that ball off or strike out,&rdquo; Gardenhire said. &ldquo;Good thing we had a veteran up there that knew the strike zone, too, and Jordy put the ball in play and got the big hit for us.

&ldquo;We had a lot of good at-bats, but when you&rsquo;re playing a team like that, you never feel comfortable, just because you know how many times they can score really quickly.&rdquo;

Indeed, the Yankees' six home runs gave them the MLB single-season record for home runs, at least for a few minutes. The Minnesota Twins later tied the Yankees for the record, after setting it at Comerica Park near the start of the month.

Still, the Yankees' home runs &mdash; a few of them tape-measure shots &mdash; were no match for  the game&rsquo;s only error, when All-Star second baseman Gleyber Torres dropped the exchange on what could have been an inning-ending double play in the Tigers&rsquo; six-run third inning.

The Yankees had the home runs. They had the stands, at least half of which were New York fans. Despite that, and 100 losses already, the Tigers hit their way to a win.

&ldquo;We beat them at our game,&rdquo; Mercer said.
