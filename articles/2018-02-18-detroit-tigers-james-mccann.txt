HEADLINE: Tigers' James McCann gains new perspective with birth of preemie twins
BYLINE: Anthony Fenech
DATE: 2018-02-18

LAKELAND, Fla. &ndash; James and Jessica McCann always had this week circled on their calendar, the week pitchers and catchers report to

Last August, the couple announced they were expecting twins. But two weeks later, they were informed the pregnancy &mdash; like many twin pregnancies &mdash; was considered high-risk. And thus began the story of their whirlwind winter, one that tested their faith and fortitude like no other.

They moved from Arkansas to Nashville. They only had one vehicle. And on Dec. 6, their twin boys, Christian and Kane, were born two months before their due date of Feb. 11.

&ldquo;I was shocked,&rdquo; Jessica said recently. &ldquo;I was not expecting it. I had an appointment on Dec. 5, and they came in and said, &lsquo;Tomorrow morning, we&rsquo;re having a C-section.' I never went into labor, just had a scheduled C-section. We were surprised to say the least.&rdquo;

The next seven weeks were spent in the neonatal intensive care unit at Vanderbilt Children&rsquo;s Hospital, mostly behind the glass as James, the Tigers' starting catcher, and Jessica watched their sons. They could hold them once a day, then for an hour a day, but the doctors made no promises.

&ldquo;I would be lying if I said I was never worried they wouldn&rsquo;t make it, just because with twins, it&rsquo;s a risky pregnancy as is,&rdquo; Jessica said. &ldquo;But I never really thought we wouldn&rsquo;t be bringing them home; but also in the back of my mind, I knew that was a possibility.&rdquo;

On Jan. 26, Christian and Kane were released from the hospital, having grown significantly from the three-plus pounds they were born at. They are now both going on seven pounds, and on Wednesday, the whole family reported to spring training in Tiger Town for what their parents hope is the first of many trips.

&ldquo;It was very important,&rdquo; Jessica said. &ldquo;The entire pregnancy, that was my goal, knowing we didn&rsquo;t know really when the babies would be born. We&rsquo;ve always been at spring training since we&rsquo;ve been married and that&rsquo;s always been so important to us to be together as a family. This was really crucial for us to make it.&rdquo;

The spring season &mdash; with day workouts to begin camp and nearly all day games on the schedule &mdash; will allow the family to have some quiet evenings while watching the babies grow. Already, they have impacted  James, who has something important to look forward to after leaving the baseball complex for the day.

&ldquo;My first reaction is, it&rsquo;s no longer my world,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;It&rsquo;s their world. But you always hear men talk about how having a child changes their lives, and yeah, you hear it and whatever, but when it actually does happen, it really does change your life and it&rsquo;s just been amazing.&rdquo;

The night before they entered the lobby of the administrative offices at the Tiger Town complex, James came home and wanted to feed both kids.

&ldquo;It&rsquo;s so funny,&rdquo; Jessica said. &ldquo;He says, &lsquo;Give me both of them,&rsquo; and he&rsquo;s over there juggling and trying to balance both bottles and both heads and it&rsquo;s pretty cool to see. But he&rsquo;s amazing. He does a really good job.&rdquo;

Said James: &ldquo;I think when you go home at night and you get to pick up not one baby, but in our situation, you get to pick up two babies, people always tell you baseball is what you do, it&rsquo;s not who you are. But when you go home at night and yeah, I have my wife, but everything is just baseball, baseball, baseball, then baseball can become who you are.

&ldquo;But when you go home and you pick up two babies and you change from the baseball player hat to the father hat, really, your mind doesn&rsquo;t get stuck on maybe the failure that you&rsquo;re dealing with or whatever&rsquo;s going on at the field. Your mind goes to being that dad.&rdquo;

But as James knows, Jessica &mdash; who spent a majority of the time at the hospital watching the children while McCann prepped for the upcoming season with workouts &mdash; is the unsung hero  of the twins&rsquo; birth.

&ldquo;I can&rsquo;t say enough about her,&rdquo; James said. &ldquo;She deserves all the credit in the world.&rdquo;

Both James and Jessica agreed it was hard to pinpoint the toughest part of being a parent, instead saying the offseason as a whole was the biggest challenge they&rsquo;ve faced in life.

&ldquo;We had anything stressful that a couple could go through, we were going through it,&rdquo; Jessica said. &ldquo;And then to throw the NICU on top of it and just the time apart and never really getting to have an off-season &ndash; to have any time for us to have together or just enjoy with the boys, which was always spent at the hospital &ndash; I would say that was the most difficult part. It was all difficult.&rdquo;

But now, with their baby boys in the stroller next to them, the emotion in their voice made it clear that all the difficulties were worth it.

Doctors have told them there are no long-term effects challenging the babies. Everything has been normal, and they passed every test administered at the hospital

&ldquo;They&rsquo;ve been doing great,&rdquo; Jessica said. &ldquo;They&rsquo;re strong little guys.&rdquo;

And their parents believe the twins were working together during the process.

&ldquo;There&rsquo;s times I noticed in the hospital, she would be tending to one of them and they&rsquo;re hooked up to their machines and one of their numbers would be going down,&rdquo; James said. &ldquo;And I would be saying, &lsquo;Breathe, you gotta breathe&rsquo; and I&rsquo;d be watching his numbers, taking care of this one and all of a sudden, this one I&rsquo;m taking care of who&rsquo;s doing fine would take a deep breath and all of a sudden those (other) numbers would jump. It happened five or six times. And he was breathing fine, there was no reason for him to do that.&rdquo;

Throughout the whole process, the McCanns had faith things would be fine.

&ldquo;Even through the whole pregnancy, with all the high risk and everything that we knew was going on, I think both of us were at peace with everything,&rdquo; James said. &ldquo;We were just able to give it over to God and put it in his hands and understand that we were placed in that situation for a reason, and whether we understand that reason now or 10 years from now or we never understand it, there&rsquo;s a reason for it. And I mean, sure, there were days that were harder than others, but I don&rsquo;t think we ever doubted the situation that we were in.&rdquo;

And no matter how difficult the situation in the hospital, perspective was gained from watching the couple next to them.

&ldquo;We met other twin parents there having a rougher time than we were and we were like, 'OK, we can&rsquo;t complain because ours are on track and doing what they&rsquo;re supposed to do,' &rdquo; Jessica said.

The babies&rsquo; names were finalized about seven hours before they were unexpectedly born. Both middle names were previously chosen &mdash; Thomas for Christian, which is James&rsquo; middle name, and Timothy for Kane, in honor of James' little brother who was a stillborn.

Christian was a name they liked for quite some time. And then with the couple deciding between a few names, James decided he was going to look up each and see if there was any meaning behind them.

The first name he looked up was Kane.

&ldquo;And it translates to &lsquo;little warrior&rsquo; and &lsquo;little fighter,&rsquo; &rdquo; James said. &ldquo;And that&rsquo;s why he got Timothy as a middle name, because he was the one that was dealing with more and he was the smaller baby and so he was the one that was having to fight.&rdquo;

For all the challenges this offseason brought the McCanns, they wouldn&rsquo;t change a thing. There were long, sleepless nights in the NICU and a cloud of doubt as to when &mdash; or even if &mdash; they would bring their boys home. But here the twins were, earlier in the week, at their first spring training.

&ldquo;There&rsquo;s a reason for it and I&rsquo;m sure the curveball that we received this offseason,&rdquo; James said, &ldquo;I&rsquo;m sure the two of them will figure out how to throw an even better curveball at some point in their lives.&rdquo;
