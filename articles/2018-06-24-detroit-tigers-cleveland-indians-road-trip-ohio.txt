Sitting by its lonesome on a couch inside the visitor&rsquo;s clubhouse after a 12-2 loss, the &ldquo;rally goose,&rdquo; perhaps for the first time questioning its ability, had no answers. Neither did the Tigers, most of whom haven&rsquo;t been around long enough to sum up the situation as accurately as right fielder Nicholas Castellanos.

&ldquo;It&rsquo;s just growth,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;You&rsquo;re either in it for the long haul or you&rsquo;re not. Getting your ass kicked is part of learning how to become a good team.&rdquo;

What started in Cincinnati with two losses to the worst team in the National League ended with a sweep in Cleveland against the AL Central leader, a three-game beating in which the Tigers were outscored 26-3.

They did not hit well. They did not pitch well. They did not play defense well. It was the junior varsity against the varsity, the script flipped from 2011-14 when the Tigers would routinely beat up on the Indians.

Castellanos was there for the end of that run. And during the long innings in right field over the weekend, the thought certainly popped into his mind, a thought that illustrates just what exactly the goal is and how far they have yet to go.

&ldquo;It&rsquo;s no secret that if we want to get to the World Series, we gotta go through Cleveland,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;And we gotta figure out how to get our stuff together, especially here, in Cleveland. I can&rsquo;t remember the last time we won a game here.&rdquo;

No loss last season was more indicative of the mental fortitude of that bunch than that one: During the game, two scouts offered the opinion that the team had quit.

&ldquo;You look back at the losses early in the year, we were right in those ballgames,&rdquo; James McCann said. &ldquo;Now&rsquo;s the time where the true character of the team will come out. It's these moments that are a true character test for what kind of team we are. For as much fight as the Tigers have showed, for as many injuries they&rsquo;ve battled through and for as much progress that has been made in fundamentals, clubhouse chemistry and leadership, another test awaits them on Monday afternoon at Comerica Park.

&ldquo;This weekend, they took it to us and we didn&rsquo;t respond,&rdquo; McCann said.

But the most important aspect of this team &mdash; the aggressive mentality that manager Ron Gardenhire has preached since spring training &mdash; is about to be challenged like it hasn&rsquo;t been before.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;ll turn the page,&rdquo; Castellanos said. &ldquo;Forget it, wash it down the shower and come out and get it tomorrow. But man, we need to get back home.&rdquo;

When they do, there will be a brand new ping-pong table waiting for them in the clubhouse. The goose, packed into a laundry bag by a Progressive Field clubhouse attendant, will assume its customary position in the Tigers&rsquo; dugout.

And we will soon see how tough this Tigers team is.
