When the Tigers beat the Orioles last week, they cut ties to that infamy. But the final historical hurdle they had to clear, the one that has stuck in manager Ron Gardenhire&rsquo;s craw all season, was still ahead.

Entering this weekend&rsquo;s series against the White Sox, the Tigers needed to win just one of their final six home games of the season to avoid becoming the first team in modern baseball history to lose 60 of their 81 home games.

On Friday night, a blowout loss. On Saturday, another loss.

But on Sunday afternoon at Comerica Park, in front of a sparse crowd constantly checking the Lions&rsquo; game their smart phones, the Tigers ensured they will not be remembered in the record books as the worst of anything.

&ldquo;Honestly, I didn&rsquo;t know how many we had to win to get that out of the way,&rdquo; manager Ron Gardenhire said.

It is likely Gardenhire was joking: Throughout the season, Gardenhire has expressed frustration with the Tigers&rsquo; struggles at home; as recently as last home stand, he talked about avoiding the 60-loss milestone.

&ldquo;But, if you&rsquo;re saying we won&rsquo;t (break the record), then I&rsquo;m fine with that. You don&rsquo;t want to do stuff like that. Setting records in a negative way is never a good thing.&rdquo;

In the win, the Tigers hit four home runs: Jeimer Candelario responded to an early deficit with a two-run home run, Gordon Beckham hit the game-winning home run, Victor Reyes went to the opposite field and Willi Castro hit his first.

&ldquo;He&rsquo;s still a young baseball player,&rdquo; Gardenhire said about Candelario, who is having a late-season surge. &ldquo;He&rsquo;s got a little more experience than some of these guys, but he&rsquo;s still a very young baseball player. We&rsquo;ve seen him hit that ball like that and hopefully, we&rsquo;ll see it a few more times.&rdquo;

But perhaps the biggest hit of the game was a two-out, opposite-field double from Harold Castro in the bottom of the third inning. Two batters later, Christin Stewart rolled a hit through the right side of the infield to score Castro and give Boyd a run of breathing room.

Boyd&rsquo;s day began by allowing a two-run home run to Eloy Jimenez, his 39th home run allowed this season, which ranks as the most in the baseball. Chicago put an average of two baserunners on per inning &ndash; Boyd allowed eight hits and two walks &ndash; but he avoided further damage until an RBI single with two outs in the top of the fifth.

&ldquo;He kind of grinded through it,&rdquo; Gardenhire said. &ldquo;I think it was a little bit of work for him. The home runs against him are just amazing, but he&rsquo;s a battler, he&rsquo;s game-on.

Boyd struck out six batters and left the game with the Tigers ahead, 5-3.

The bullpen kept it there, throwing four scoreless innings: John Schreiber struck out four batters in 1⅔ innings, left-hander Nick Ramirez left a runner on base on a strikeout to end the seventh inning and closer Joe Jimenez picked up his eighth save.

In the win, the Tigers totaled 12 hits. Dawel Lugo paced the Tigers with three hits. Candelario hit his eighth home run, Beckham his sixth, and for Reyes &ndash; who continues to live near the .300 batting average mark &ndash; it was his second.
