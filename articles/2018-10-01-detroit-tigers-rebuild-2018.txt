It was an unsatisfying story, but

There was no comeback on this day, nothing to indicate the small but significant strides the team made in their first full rebuilding year. There was only the reality that a winning season  is still years away.

Nicholas Castellanos stayed seated when the final out was recorded, as Dawel Lugo mercifully swung at a first pitch and grounded out to shortstop at 5:36 p.m. local time, closing this chapter of change in Tigers history.

Castellanos leaned over the railing at the far end of the dugout and sat there for minutes as his teammates filtered into the clubhouse as losers one final time.

He thought about the season, what went good, what went wrong, &ldquo;non-biased reflecting&rdquo; as he called it, and more than once, looked at the other side, where the Brewers &mdash;considered a rebuilding team as recently as 2016 &mdash; saluted their home fans before heading to Chicago to play for a division title on Monday afternoon.

&ldquo;We&rsquo;re not on that side,&rdquo; Castellanos said afterwards, sharing some of those thoughts as he packed up his locker. &ldquo;We don&rsquo;t have a tomorrow. You&rsquo;re frustrated when you&rsquo;re on this side. It&rsquo;s the last game of the season and music&rsquo;s not playing, everything is quiet, so it&rsquo;s not an environment that I&rsquo;m happy in, but it&rsquo;s the reality of the situation.&rdquo;

And the reality of the Tigers&rsquo; situation is that it could get worse next season. They will be just as young and just as impotent at the plate, once again dependent on unpolished players with their best prospects still years away.

Rebuilding is not an single-year process, nor does it often take two or three years, even if you&rsquo;re doing it right. And the Tigers, by stomaching 98 losses this year, are doing it right. There still are no guarantees that it will work, not with the luck needed in the process.

The first building block was new manager Ron Gardenhire and his veteran, tightly knit coaching staff changing the culture inside a clubhouse that was broken by the end of last season. They know baseball and they know how to teach it; they won over the players early, despite the impending losses.

"We're going to rebuild this thing, starting from ground level," Gardenhire said. "As long as we can keep them playing hard and start paying attention to the details of the game, we'll be OK."

After the last of those losses, the players prepared to go their separate ways, long since resigned to their fate, which Castellanos wore frustratingly on his face.

&ldquo;As a competitor, you have the highest of expectations,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;Shock the world, prove everybody wrong, make the haters eat their words.

&ldquo;This proves that they&rsquo;re right and you&rsquo;re the one that&rsquo;s wrong. It&rsquo;s frustrating, but like I said, it is what it is.&rdquo;

Approaching a career crossroads &mdash; Castellanos is one year away from free agency and will be available on the trade market this winter &mdash; he turned in the best season of his career, hitting .298 with 23 home runs and 89 RBIs in a lineup that offered no protection while learning to play right field.

He assumed a leadership position and held it well, the evidence of that coming after the final out. There was a face-to-face with JaCoby Jones, reminding him of his potential. A comment to Victor Reyes about his future as a No. 3 hitter. A trip around the clubhouse on the way to the shower, shaking hands with nearly every one of his teammates.

His reflection in the dugout told him that, as a player, he's &ldquo;still inconsistent,&rdquo; and that as a leader, he gets &ldquo;selfish at times when I&rsquo;m struggling.&rdquo;

Asked for the main takeaway of those thoughts, he said, &ldquo;The Tigers, in general, we have a lot of work to do. Me, personally, I have a lot of work to do.&rdquo;

The work needed was apparent Sunday: The Tigers played sloppily, making mistakes in their head and on the field; in the sixth inning, Castellanos couldn&rsquo;t lay off a slider out of the zone, just as the Brewers had expected.

&ldquo;A bunch of young kids haven&rsquo;t played like veterans for the whole season,&rdquo; he said. &ldquo;That&rsquo;s the challenge. It&rsquo;s tough to do that when you don&rsquo;t have the experience of the season, so it was a growing process.&rdquo;

The growing process will continue into next season, with or without Castellanos. Some of those young players will have a season under their belt; others, just a few months. That experience should be invaluable to their development.

The Tigers played better than expected this season. They were competitive in many games, despite losing their best hitter and pitcher to injuries. They lost 30 one-run games &mdash; and even more by larger margins &mdash; by not doing the small things.

Still, they were respectable and proved to be a tough out against the better teams. Said Astros manager A.J. Hinch, after sweeping the Tigers by three runs combined over three games earlier in the month: &ldquo;I wouldn&rsquo;t expect anything less from a Gardenhire-managed team.&rdquo;

But being "better than expected" still resulted in 98 losses, leaving the Tigers 27 games out of the division lead.

Asked if he thought the team was closer to playing in October than people expect, Castellanos said, &ldquo;In a lot of ways, yeah,&rdquo; citing the top pitching prospects that are likely to arrive in Detroit in the coming years.

But in the most important way &mdash; having major league talent &mdash; the Tigers are no closer to returning to the postseason than they were at the end of last year.

Castellanos came up playing on Tigers teams regularly expecting to reach the postseason. He knows what the postseason is like,  and the feeling of not going, even as it grows familiar, doesn&rsquo;t get any better.

&ldquo;It is what it is,&rdquo; he said, again.

Castellanos will return to South Florida on Monday, start working on his house and get some groceries. It's time for him to resume his duties as a dad. It&rsquo;s part of the reason he stayed alone in the dugout for so long, to take it in before washing off the loss in the shower. The reality stings, but it is what it is: There is no happy tomorrow for the Tigers, and there won&rsquo;t be for a while.
