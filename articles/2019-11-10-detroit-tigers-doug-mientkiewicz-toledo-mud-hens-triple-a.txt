BYLINE: Anthony Fenech

After the 2018 season &mdash; a successful one &mdash;

Mientkiewicz, considered a potential big-league manager, had just led the Mud Hens to the International League playoffs for the first time in over a decade.

Littlefield pressed for a two-year contract extension. Mientkiewicz, aware other opportunities could arise, preferred to go season-by-season. Still, he wanted to see the Tigers&rsquo; rebuilding through, out of loyalty to Tigers manager Ron Gardenhire and the coaching staff which largely molded him as a player with the Twins.

Littlefield&rsquo;s message, according to Mientkiewicz: &ldquo;We love what you did. You took a team that wasn&rsquo;t very good and made the playoffs. You did everything great.&rdquo;

&ldquo;Then what happened?&rdquo; Mientkiewicz said. &ldquo;What happened? How could you go from hiring me to then giving me a two-year deal, to now, all of a sudden, you can&rsquo;t even work with me? It just doesn&rsquo;t make sense to me.&rdquo;

Mientkiewicz, 45, will not return as Mud Hens manager in 2020, Tigers general manager Al Avila confirmed this week. In two seasons, Mientkiewicz went 139-140.

&ldquo;We appreciate Doug&rsquo;s commitment over the past two years,&rdquo; Avila said. &ldquo;We just felt it was time to go in a different direction.&rdquo;

Avila declined comment when asked for specifics on Mientkiewicz&rsquo;s dismissal. Littlefield also declined comment.

Though many minor-league coaching changes are made across baseball every year, the 180-degree turn the Tigers took with Mientkiewicz raises questions. Chief among them: Why did Mientkiewicz &mdash; a trusted confidant of Gardenhire &mdash; fall out of favor in just a year&rsquo;s time?

From his perspective, his opposition to premature big-league promotions was not welcome, push-back against daily duties unrelated to coaching was frowned upon and, finally, the organization preferred more of a "yes man" personality.

&ldquo;The problem is, you need people that are going to tell you, &lsquo;No, that&rsquo;s not really a good idea,&rsquo; &rdquo; Mientkiewicz said. &ldquo; &lsquo;You need people to say, &lsquo;Hey, look, I&rsquo;m not fighting you. At the end of the day, I&rsquo;m really trying to help you.&rsquo;

&ldquo;I told them, &lsquo;I know what (Gardenhire) wants this to look like. So, either you gotta get on board or you gotta get rid of us. Because we&rsquo;re fighting for the same thing, which is what you fail to realize. I&rsquo;m here to make your team better.&rsquo;&rdquo;

But perhaps the organization &mdash; well within its rights to change course &mdash; decided Mientkiewicz&rsquo;s fire was too much for a young team, especially with top prospects likely to arrive in 2020.

And, make no mistake, Mientkiewicz was in place to work for the front office. That the Tigers will have to pay two Triple-A managers next season &mdash; Mientkiewicz will get paid his 2020 salary unless he's hired somewhere else &mdash; speaks to their desire for a change.

Mientkiewicz is not considered a "yes man." He spent 12 seasons in the major leagues, most notably with the Twins, earning a reputation as a hard-nosed player. He admitted he was not shy about offering his opinions this season, perhaps to a detriment, while managing an early wave of prospects such as center fielder Daz Cameron, shortstop Willi Castro, catcher Jake Rogers and starting pitchers Beau Burrows and Kyle Funkhouser.

&ldquo;I&rsquo;ve done this twice now,&rdquo; said Mientkiewicz, who went through a rebuild as a player with the Twins and also for five seasons as a minor league manager from 2013-17 with the Twins. &ldquo;Trust me, you have to beat on these kids from the minute they get here &mdash; 'Are you mentally tough enough?'

&ldquo;Because you have to be mentally tough to get to the big leagues, but it takes a different M.O., a different demeanor to go, &lsquo;You know what, I&rsquo;m tired of this, I&rsquo;m not going to be like everybody else who just comes here, bides his time until I get to be a free agent and go somewhere else to get called up to the big leagues and not get our ass kicked. It takes a special kid.&rdquo;

During the Tigers' rebuilding process, the organization has heavily marketed top prospects as part of a &ldquo;Road To Detroit&rdquo; campaign. It's a move that some in the minor leagues &mdash; Mientkiewicz included &mdash; believe puts too much pressure on players who need to focus on becoming big-league ready.

&ldquo;It just turns my stomach when they tell me to call guys up that definitely weren&rsquo;t deserving,&rdquo; Mientkiewicz said. &ldquo;You have to understand &mdash; I&rsquo;m part of a fraternity in that, I know what I had to do to get called up to the big leagues and I know what I had to do to stay.

&ldquo;So, pardon me when I&rsquo;m a little perturbed that we keep calling these guys who aren&rsquo;t ready yet up, because we don&rsquo;t have anybody else.&rdquo;

Both Castro and Rogers made their MLB debuts this season: Castro hit .230 with one home run and eight RBIs in 100 at-bats; Rogers hit .125 with four home runs and eight RBIs in 112 at-bats.

No player's performance presaged the organization&rsquo;s souring on Mientkiewicz more than Cameron, who struggled in his second go-round in Toledo. Cameron hit .234 over the first two months of the season. In mid-June, Mientkiewicz suggested that the organization demote Cameron to Double-A Erie for two weeks to receive a breather.

&ldquo;Just to get his feet back on the ground,&rdquo; Mientkiewicz said. &ldquo;Just to exhale. He more than needs it, he wants it. He can&rsquo;t even sleep.&rdquo;

The Tigers declined Mientkiewicz's request, saying that factors beyond a player&rsquo;s performance go into such decisions and citing his strong spring training performance. (Cameron hit .357 with seven extra-base hits in 28 at-bats.) The translation: The organization didn't want to take a hit in public perception for demoting a key prospect, even if it stood to benefit the player.

Multiple players &mdash; all of whom said their experiences with Mientkiewicz were positive &mdash; agreed that he may not be the right fit for a younger team.

Mientkiewicz profiles as an acquired taste, one whose no-frills, full-steam-ahead demeanor could go south quickly &mdash; which, apparently, it did with Detroit.

But it's tough not to think the Tigers need a challenging voice that will spread Gardenhire's teachings throughout the minor leagues That's what the front office bought into when hiring Mientkiewicz three years ago.

But maybe they didn&rsquo;t. Or perhaps they want to go in a different direction.

Mientkiewicz&rsquo;s dismissal &mdash; though not a major move &mdash; illustrates a major question within the organization: Do they want people who are going to make a difference, or do they want people to nod their heads and keep doing what they&rsquo;re doing?
